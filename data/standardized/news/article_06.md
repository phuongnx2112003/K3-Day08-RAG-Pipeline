**Calvin C. Newport** is an American nonfiction author and full time professor of computer science at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University "Georgetown University").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-1) He is known for his writings about [attention management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_management "Attention management") in the digital age.
## Background and education
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cal_Newport&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Background and education")]
Newport grew up in [Pennington, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennington,_New_Jersey "Pennington, New Jersey"). His grandfather, John Newport, was a Baptist minister and theologian.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-2)
Calvin graduated from [Hopewell Valley Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopewell_Valley_Central_High_School "Hopewell Valley Central High School") in 2000.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-4) He completed his undergraduate studies at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College "Dartmouth College") in 2004 and received a Ph.D. in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science") from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology "Massachusetts Institute of Technology") in 2009 under [Nancy Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Lynch "Nancy Lynch").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-6) He was a post-doctoral associate in the MIT computer science department from 2009 to 2011.
## Career
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cal_Newport&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Career")]
Newport joined [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University "Georgetown University") as an assistant professor of computer science in 2011, was granted tenure in 2017, and was promoted to full professorship in 2024. His work focuses on [distributed algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_algorithms "Distributed algorithms") in challenging networking scenarios and incorporates the study of [communications systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_system "Communications system") in nature.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-7) Newport is currently Provost's Distinguished Associate Professor in the Department of Computer Science[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-8) at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University "Georgetown University") and the author of eight books.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-9)
### Attention management
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cal_Newport&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Attention management")]
See also: [Attention management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_management "Attention management")
Newport started the _Study Hacks_ blog in 2007 where he writes about "how to perform productive, valuable and meaningful work in an increasingly distracted digital age".[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-10)
Newport used the term _**deep work**_ , previously used in a psychological [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-11) or religious sense,[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-12) in his book _Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World_ (2016). Newport uses it to refer to studying or working for focused chunks of time on intellectually skilful tasks without [distractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distraction "Distraction") such as email and social media.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-13) He challenges the belief that participation in [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media "Social media") is important for career capital.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-14)
In 2017, he began advocating for "digital [minimalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimalism "Minimalism")."[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-15) In 2021, he began referring to the role [email](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email "Email") and [chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_chat "Online chat")[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-16) play in what he calls "the hyperactive hive mind".[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-17)
In addition to his blog and newsletter, he is the creator of a regular podcast about productivity and knowledge working.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-18)
In 2019, Newport released his book _Digital Minimalism: Choosing a Focused Life in a Noisy World_.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-19) During a 2019 interview with [_GQ_ magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ "GQ"), Newport suggested that our smartphones are like cigarettes and stated, "You're gonna look at allowing a 13-year-old to have a smartphone the same way that you would look at allowing your 13-year-old to smoke a cigarette."[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Work#cite_note-20)
